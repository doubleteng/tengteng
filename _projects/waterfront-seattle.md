These studies formed part of the 2013–2014 schematic design for Pier 62/63 and a proposed pool barge on Seattle’s waterfront. The wider Waterfront Program extended across 26 city blocks, from Pioneer Square to Belltown, connecting parks, paths, public spaces, and access to Elliott Bay.

The schematic proposal reshaped the existing 1.7-acre pier into approximately 1.5 acres of public open space. Surrounded by water on three sides, it linked the promenade and proposed Overlook Walk to the Seattle Aquarium and nearby waterfront destinations. Furnishings, gathering areas, and a raised walkway developed different ways to occupy the pier and look across the bay.

A gangway and floating dock connected the pier’s southern edge to the proposed pool barge, positioned approximately 65 feet away. This separation was intended to reduce the combined shadow of pier and vessel, limit the apparent scale of the barge, and create a more sheltered area of water between them. These were environmental design intentions within the schematic study.

An open circulation spine organizes the pool facilities, while a translucent bathhouse enclosure maintains views through the vessel. The barge’s dimensions also respond to the clearance of the Ballard Locks, allowing relocation for maintenance or alternative programming.

Working with Michael Mariano, John Feit, and Emily Woods at Schemata Workshop, I contributed conceptual and schematic design ideas, presentation graphics, physical models, 3D renderings, and design drawings. The images document the schematic proposal.
