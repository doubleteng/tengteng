contribution: Loop First, Void Second establishes an order of design decisions. Map the city’s forces and the needs of
  different users; construct a continuous route in plan and section; then position gathering spaces, voids, program,
  and enclosure around that route. My lectures use OMA’s circulation and program strategies to make this process legible
  and testable.
method_steps:
- title: Read the city and its users
  text: Map market, transit, parking, service, and adjacent-building edges. Write separate movement scenarios for artists,
    everyday visitors, ticketed audiences, and staff, including busy and quiet periods.
- title: Establish a sectional loop
  text: Locate a clear street entrance and a return to the city. Connect display, gathering, making overlooks, and roof
    or terrace destinations. Test level changes and public/private thresholds in plan and section together.
- title: Turn movement into encounter
  text: Alternate passage with pause. Widen landings into rooms, place seating beside the flow, and connect the route
    to shared voids at selected levels. Give the public views into creative work while protecting artists’ access and
    retreat.
- title: Resolve and communicate the building
  text: Develop program, structure, daylight, and enclosure around the spatial sequence. Use diagrams to explain decisions,
    plans and sections to verify their relationships, and renderings and models to examine occupation and atmosphere.

<h2 id="course-and-brief" style="scroll-margin-top:105px">Interior space as an urban relationship</h2>

ARC 2124, Integrated Design Studio II, studies light, color, texture, volume, and human scale through the relationship between architecture and interior space. It carries the site sensitivity developed in Integrated Design Studio I into the interior and prepares students for the tectonic integration of Integrated Design Studio III. Drawing, digital studies, and physical making support an iterative design process.

## Artists’ Collective, Cleveland

The main project transforms and expands an existing building beside West Side Market into a rotating-residency artists’ collective. Living, making, exhibition, and performance must coexist with market crowds, transit arrivals, and service access. The shared course brief calls for sectional thinking, circulation as social infrastructure, and carefully layered public/private thresholds.

In my studio section, Loop First, Void Second turns this agenda into a sequence of design decisions. Students begin with the city’s flows and the behavior of different users, establish a credible route through the building, and then develop its rooms, shared voids, and enclosure. Two initial prototypes guide testing: a market-to-roof promenade, and a through-block passage connected to a vertical loop. Both require a legible arrival, places to pause, selective views into making, and a return to the city.

Plans and sections must make the route spatially convincing. Diagrams explain how it is organized; renderings and models examine what it feels like to move, gather, work, and look across levels. The selected work below brings these forms of evidence together for each project.
