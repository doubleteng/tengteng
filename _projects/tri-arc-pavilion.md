## Staged post-tensioning in a full-scale polyhedral 3DGS timber canopy

Polyhedral three-dimensional graphic statics (3DGS) generates spatial form and force simultaneously through reciprocal diagrams. Its line-based equilibrium network does not resolve the effects of finite timber sections, joint rigidity, fabrication tolerances, construction sequence, compliant foundations, or wind-uplift load reversal. These conditions can introduce bending and instability as a network becomes a building.

TRI-ARC investigates post-tensioning as a method for preserving and activating an intended force state during materialization. The research distinguishes primary cables, which contribute to global equilibrium and uplift resistance, from secondary cables, which control local cantilever behavior.

## From reciprocal diagrams to prefabricated trusses

The design began without a predetermined architectural form. Polyhedral 3DGS generated three mutually supporting arches arranged as a triangle in plan. Twelve prefabricated timber trusses materialize this network, with member cross-sections differentiated according to force demand.

Plasma-cut steel plates sit within CNC-milled recesses in the timber. Plate geometry was scaled according to local finite-element stress demand. Because excavation was prohibited, the three supports bear on concrete ballast blocks.

## Four construction states

The assembly sequence separates the untensioned timber frame, the primary-cable state, the secondary-cable state, and the completed canopy. Structural post-tensioning preceded installation of the non-prestressed fabric cladding. This order separates the cables’ stabilizing action from the environmental loads collected by the fabric.

Primary cables run along the arch systems and connect to the ballasted foundations. Secondary cables support the extended cantilevered members. Snow and wind-uplift loads are assigned from the actual fabric-panel areas to adjoining timber members.

## Structural evaluation framework

The evaluation compares the ideal 3DGS network with nonlinear beam-and-cable models of the four construction states under self-weight, snow, symmetric uplift, and asymmetric uplift. Measures include global displacement, cantilever-tip deflection, cable forces, support reactions, member and connection utilization, and the relative axial and bending strain energy.

Sensitivity studies examine cable prestress, joint rotational stiffness, and support compliance. An as-built geometric survey and non-destructive field loading provide the calibration route between the materialized model and the completed structure. The staged comparison is the basis for quantifying each cable hierarchy’s effect; numerical performance values are not reported here.

## An adjustable built force state

TRI-ARC connects equilibrium-based form generation to differentiated timber sizing, concealed connections, staged cable tensioning, and ballasted foundations. Treating the force state as adjustable during assembly extends the design problem beyond finding a geometry: the structure must establish and retain its intended behavior as materials, joints, supports, and environmental loads enter the system.
