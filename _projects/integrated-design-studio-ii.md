method_steps:
- title: Map arrivals and users
  text: Trace movement from transit, market entrances, streets, and parking. Identify where visitors, artists, and
    service teams meet or need separation.
- title: Draw the loop in section
  text: Connect entry, exhibition, gathering, making, and a return to the street. Test level changes, accessible
    routes, and public/private thresholds in plan and section together.
- title: Give the route places to pause
  text: Widen landings into rooms and place atria, courtyards, or overlooks where paths meet. Use visibility to
    connect visitors with creative work while protecting studios and dwellings.
- title: Develop and test the building
  text: Organize program, structure, and envelope around the route. Compare drawings with physical models to check
    daylight, human scale, continuity, and the relationship between retained fabric and new construction.

<h2 id="course-and-brief" style="scroll-margin-top:105px">Interior space as an urban relationship</h2>

ARC 2124, Integrated Design Studio 2, examines light, material, volume, and human scale through the relationship between architecture and interior space. Students carry site analysis into the building and develop their ideas through drawings, digital studies, and physical models. The course connects ID1’s emphasis on site and landscape with the tectonic integration of ID3.

## Artists’ Collective, Cleveland

The main project transforms and expands an existing building beside the West Side Market into a rotating-residency artists’ collective. Living, making, exhibition, and performance share a site shaped by market visitors, transit arrivals, parking, and service access. Students must invite public participation while giving artists places to work and retreat.

My section approaches this brief through Loop First, Void Second. Students establish how people enter, move, pause, and return to the city before fixing the building’s major volumes. Two initial prototypes offer different tests: a market-to-roof promenade linking public destinations across levels, and a through-block passage joined to a vertical loop. Atria and courtyards then give the route daylight, orientation, and places for encounter.
